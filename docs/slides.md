# Slide 1 - Actual system scope

- The LSTM learns `initial_access`, `lateral_movement`, and `command_and_control`.
- Reconnaissance (`TA0043`) and exfiltration (`TA0010`) use separate rule-based signals, not the learned model.
- PCAP is parsed with Scapy into NetFlow-style columns and then fed through the same host pipeline.
- Those PCAP features approximate the NetFlow-V3 training features and were only tested on bundled synthetic captures.

Suggested visual: a simple flow-to-host pipeline with a separate heuristic branch for recon/exfil.

# Slide 2 - Host forecast pipeline

- Flows are aggregated into 60-second source-host windows with `min_flows=3` and top-host filtering.
- A 10-window host sequence feeds the LSTM and a `K=5` future rollout.
- The network alarm is the highest host attack probability in the window and is not calibrated.
- Current detection uses the last three observed windows; the future risk is a later host-sequence forecast.

Suggested visual: 60-second host windows feeding the LSTM with a forecast horizon bar.

# Slide 3 - Rules and caveats

- Recon rules look for scan-shape and no-response patterns; exfil rules look for a large outbound concentration to one destination.
- The repo reports the heuristic rules fired on hosts without dataset labels in some labelled samples; the status is unresolved.
- The rules were only validated on synthetic captures generated by `scripts/generate_sample_pcap.py`, not on a labelled PCAP dataset.
- The same rules are not in the benchmark, and they are not treated as learned stages.

Suggested visual: a small recon/exfil rule card with a note on synthetic-only validation.

# Slide 4 - What the benchmark says

- Host-level macro-F1 favors persistence at h3 (`0.713145` vs `0.576085`), while the LSTM has much lower FPR (`0.006246` vs `0.098204`).
- In the brute-force sample, the LSTM predicts `command_and_control`, and the active window probabilities reach `0.962` to `0.985` as the highest host probability in the window.
- Host-level onset values from `benchmark_v3_host.json` are small precision and moderate recall, and the network view is the earlier representation in `benchmark_v3.json`.
- The identity diagnostic reports FPR 0.0054077578 for never-attack hosts and 0.0580991161 for ever-attack hosts; this is not a claim of generalised unseen-host performance.

Suggested visual: paired metric bars and a small onset table.

# Slide 5 - Practical use and limits

- The system is decision support for host prioritisation with explicit uncertainty, not an autonomous verdict.
- It is designed to separate current evidence, forecast evidence, and heuristic context from learned-stage output.
- The benchmark is on one dataset and uses proxy labels; budgeted ablation and leave-one-attack-out procedures are not full-data comparisons.
- [MISSING] marks the absence of a real labelled PCAP benchmark or a confirmed production deployment result.
